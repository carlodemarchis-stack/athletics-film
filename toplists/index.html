The All-Time Film

A guy with a scarf

One card per event, women's reel and men's reel, in the order World Athletics lists them. Each card holds the twenty best marks ever in that event — every performance, not one per athlete, so the same name can appear again and again. That repetition is the point: Mondo Duplantis owns nineteen of the twenty best pole vaults ever cleared.

Because marks tie, twenty is a rank and not a row count. Everyone ranked twentieth or better is here, so a card may carry 21, 23, even 30 marks — the women's high jump has thirty, since so many share 2.06. Tied marks keep World Athletics' shared rank and the repeated number is dimmed.

The ladder on the left ranks them, each bar a name, one colour per athlete. The chart on the right puts the same marks where they happened in time, which is where each event tells on itself: the men's marathon list is almost entirely the last ten years, the women's shot put has not admitted a new mark since 1987.

Marks are wind-legal and electronically timed. An i beside a mark means it was set indoors. The bar at the top of the card shows who owns the list.
